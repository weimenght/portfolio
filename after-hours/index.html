People aren't the means.
People are the point.

After work I make small, free things that try to take that seriously: a jar, a card a day, a few flowers for someone, a house of books to borrow, a home your people build together, a table for New Year's Eve, a walk at the same time, a town, a train window, a table for two far apart, a way to stay close, a few words in someone else's language. None of them want more of your time. They'd rather you spent it with someone.

Why I do all this

By day I design learning for teams. Again and again I see the same thing: what changes people isn't the content, it's the moment two of them actually talk.

Screens are getting very good at keeping us. I keep asking the opposite question: can a screen send us back to each other, a little warmer than before?

So after work I build small things to find out. A jar that sends you to cook for someone. A town that sends you off with “go and see someone you love”. A way for two people in different cities to share a morning.

And I want to explore how AI can serve that purpose: not to keep us on the screen, but to bring us back to where we should be. I build them with AI as a tool; the people they're for are the point.

If one of them makes you call someone, it did its job.

— Wei

How I design them

Five rules I keep

  1. 01It ends off the screen.Every visit closes with something to do away from it: call someone, cook, go outside, sleep.
  2. 02Nothing to win.No scores, streaks or badges. Nothing counts how long you stayed.
  3. 03Made for two.Most things work alone or with someone far away, from one link.
  4. 04Yours stays yours.No ads, no tracking, sign-in only where it has to be. Most of it never leaves your device.
  5. 05Small on purpose.One thing at a time. A short visit is a good visit.
