NeuroLeadership Core: The brain treats social threats like physical dangers. Detect which domain is threatened and shift from threat defensiveness to an inquiry-led reward.
S Status (Relative Importance)

Threat: Public critique, micro-checking, uncredited contributions.

"What insights from your expertise should guide this?"
C Certainty (Predicting Outcomes)

Threat: Shifting roadmaps, ambiguous reorgs, opaque expectations.

"What are the 3 constants we can firmly anchor on?"
A Autonomy (Sense of Control)

Threat: Handing down rigid solutions, dictating step-by-step methods.

"Here is the deliverable outcome. How would you design the path?"
R Relatedness (Belonging & Safety)

Threat: Silos, friend-to-boss role alienation, feeling isolated.

"How can I back you up so you do not feel alone in this?"
F Fairness (Perception of Equity)

Threat: Credit claimed by others, shifting goalposts, favoritism.

"What objective rubric makes this completely transparent?"
The Coaching Habit: Say less, ask more. Tame your manager's "Advice Monster" by deploying Stanier's 7 high-leverage questions. Click any question to paste or test directly:
1. The Kickstart Question

"What's on your mind?"

2. The AWE Question (Best in the World)

"And what else?"

3. The Focus Question (Game Changer)

"What's the real challenge here for you?"

4. The Foundation Question

"What do you want?"

5. The Lazy Question

"How can I help you?"

6. The Strategic Question

"If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"

7. The Learning Question

"What was most useful for you here?"

Hersey-Blanchard SLII: Diagnose developmental stage for a specific task and calibrate your supportive vs. directive behavior:
D1: Enthusiastic Beginner S1: Directing

Low Competence, High Commitment. Needs clear steps, templates, timelines, and frequent check-ins.

D2: Disillusioned Learner S2: Coaching

Low Competence, Low Commitment. Hit roadblocks. Needs empathy, attribution shift, and collaborative problem-solving.

D3: Capable but Cautious S3: Supporting

High Competence, Variable Commitment. Fear of failure or impostor syndrome. Needs safety, validation, and sounding board.

D4: Self-Reliant Achiever S4: Delegating

High Competence, High Commitment. Autonomous mastery. Needs executive autonomy, blindspot checks, and room to innovate.

SBI Situation • Behavior • Impact

Prevents defensiveness by stripping subjective judgments and anchoring strictly on observable, verifiable facts:

  • Situation: Exact anchor in time and location.
  • Behavior: Observable physical action (not attitude).
  • Impact: The verifiable team or business outcome.
RC Radical Candor (Kim Scott)

Balancing authentic personal care with unambiguous direct challenge:

Ruinous Empathy: Caring personally but refusing to challenge directly (leads to underperformance).
Radical Candor: Caring personally AND challenging directly with clear constructive truth.
