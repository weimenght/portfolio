    The story

    One invented company. One year. Thirteen conversations that would not have happened otherwise.

    Ana, an engineer promoted to manager in January · Leo, her most senior report · Mei, the graduate who joins in September · Sam, the MD who is always sure · Priya, who leads the Bengaluru team on the Monday call · Jordi, in sales · Nadia, in HR, who gets every request for training · Tomás, in product.

    The names are the ones the tools already use. Any likeness to a real company is the point, not an accident.

    I / The ask

    Listen before you train.

    January to March

    Nadia in HR gets a request: "the team leads need feedback training". The diagnosis points somewhere else: Ana, promoted in January, is running her first 1:1s like stand-ups.

    Ep. 01 · Act I
    01 — Nadia reads the request aloud.

    “Someone has asked for a training.”

    Most requests are about expectations, feedback, tools or incentives, not skill. We open the two-minute diagnosis with the requester, put their own numbers through the cost calculator, and keep Room Mode and the teach-back for the day a programme is right.

    II / The room goes quiet

    The senior person goes first.

    April to June

    A near-miss nobody reports. Priya's team listens on the Monday call. A decision made in the meeting and remade in the car park. Three functions blame each other for one delay. Then Sam, the MD who is always sure, goes first with a mistake.

    Ep. 04 · Act II
    04 — Sam hangs the first exhibit.

    “Nobody says anything in the meeting, then everything in the corridor.”

    Silence is a learned reading of consequences; it unlearns the same way: the senior person goes first, and it costs nothing. Mistake Museum, Question Deck as a weekly ritual, and the sector versions: Near-Miss Wall for the toolbox talk, Second Opinion for the dealing desk.

    III / New people, new tools

    Played, not presented.

    July to October

    Mei's first day is played, not presented; her day-30 with Ana happens in the stockroom. The AI licences arrive and Leo shows Mei one real use. Jordi stops pitching early. Tomás's product team interviews a real colleague.

    Ep. 08 · Act III
    08 — Mei's first day, played.

    “Onboarding is a handbook and a deck.”

    The first day was presented, not played, and the first month had no conversation in it. Launchpad for the day itself (route cards, station tools, a colleague trail to real people, a wall with no podium), Day Thirty for every manager in week five, Teach-Back Chain so every joiner teaches the next.

    09 — Leo shows Mei one real use.

    “We bought the AI licences; usage is 20%.”

    The barrier is social: people are afraid of looking slow or replaceable. Prompt Partner (your colleague plays the AI, no AI inside), Show Me Yours (each person shows one real use or one thing they gave up on; the chain carries it to a third person), and the diagnosis before anyone books a course.

    Ep. 10 · Act III
    10 — Jordi stays curious a little longer.

    “Reps pitch too early and freeze on objections.”

    The stated request is rarely the real need, and knowing a technique in a workshop isn't recalling it under pressure. Discovery Duet (a client with a hidden need, a pitch bell, no score), and the Sales Academy rebuilt as a pair drill: spaced cards with honest evidence ratings, then a colleague reads the objection and judges whether the reply would land.

    IV / After the cut

    True, not true, or by a date.

    November

    A restructure. The people who stay go quiet. Ana answers only "true", "not true", or "I don't know yet, and I'll tell you by a date".

    Ep. 13 · Act IV
    13 — Ana answers only three ways.

    “The team has gone quiet since the restructure.”

    Trust is rebuilt one honest exchange at a time; reassurance nobody asked for makes it worse. What's True Now for every team lead in the week after (anonymous questions; the manager may answer only "true", "not true", or "I don't know yet, and I'll tell you by a date"), the Co-Pilot to rehearse the hardest one, Question Deck to keep the weekly five minutes.
