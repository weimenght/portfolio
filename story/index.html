It's all about people

Wei Meng · 7 Nov 2026

I'm Wei. This is my story.

I grew up in a small town in northern China, where my parents, and their parents, have lived their whole lives. At seventeen I left for university, the first in my family to go. It was my first glimpse of life in a big city. Only a glimpse.

I spent four years studying, without much thought, the subjects meant to make me an engineer. They turned out to be of little use, because I never meant to become one. My days went to reading: Wang Xiaobo, A Cheng, Italo Calvino, Gabriel García Márquez. Their minds were open and inventive, and I wanted mine to be like that.

I was also in love. I followed her to Beijing. I had no idea I'd stay more than ten years.

· · ·

I tried different kinds of work. First I was an editor at a publishing house, because I loved reading. I met many creative people there. I also burned out there: day after day of reading, editing, working, because back then I thought that was my only purpose.

My second job was at ByteDance, a company setting out to win the world through screens, by capturing attention. I started as a content moderator and soon moved to the training team. That's where my career began to take shape. At a Chinese company with a global reach, I worked with colleagues all over the world. Some of them are still good friends.

· · ·

There I had my first breakdown. My girlfriend of more than six years left me. It made me realise I was bad at it. Bad at love, bad at being a partner. The most important subject there is, and no one had ever taught me.

I buried myself in work. Glamorous global training programmes, huge budgets. It was an era of spending freely, and I had the privilege of watching what training actually does to people's work, and to the state they work in. Plot twist: it isn't the money that matters. It's whether two people end up really talking.

After ByteDance I moved to Deloitte, to see a new sector and a different approach to learning and development. There I ran more in-person, multi-day programmes, and studied coaching as a method. Things moved slowly. My nerves could rest. But somewhere along the way I lost my sense of direction, however much I learned about the world.

Until one day I couldn't move. Not even a little. I asked for professional help and learned it was depression. That was the start of my fight against inertia. My girlfriend at the time offered help, but the sudden change overwhelmed her too.

That was when I made a new rule for living: whatever I did had one purpose, to make today work. I had no strength to spare for anything else. It forced me to live in the moment. It forced me to go to my friends. It forced me to see what matters in my life.

· · ·

Two years on, I'm somewhere completely different. The magic of AI opened my eyes: suddenly I could build what I'd only imagined, things that bring people back to each other instead of keeping them on screens. A new love sparked a new way of living. And I've come closer to what I want to do: help us thrive. At work, where we spend a third of our lives. And after hours, with a clear mind, an energetic body and the people we love around us.

FREE//BUILDER and after-hours grew from one idea: technology should bring us closer to real people and real life. KPIs, scores and algorithms are what trap us and hijack our attention. They drive the business model. But they aren't the only way, and they shouldn't be.

This is what I believe. People are the point. When we're at our best, we do what's best for the people around us. We don't work for companies; we work with people. We don't live alone; we live with people.

It's all about people.
